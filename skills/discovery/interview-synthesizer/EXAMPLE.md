# Example: interview-synthesizer

## Input

> Here are notes from 6 interviews with freelance designers about invoicing.
> [excerpts] P1: "I just use a Google Doc template I copy each time." P2: "I forget
> to chase late payers — last month I was owed $4k and only noticed when rent was
> due." P3: "I'd love an AI that does my taxes." P4: "I built a spreadsheet that
> flags overdue invoices, took me a weekend." P5: "Chasing clients feels rude so I
> put it off." P6: "I use Wave but the reminders are too aggressive, scared a
> client off." Synthesize.

## Output

# Freelancer Invoicing — Interview Synthesis

## Method & sample
6 freelance designers, convenience sample, single round. Small n — directional, not conclusive. Some questions may have been leading (noted below).

## Themes

**1. Chasing late payments is the real pain — and it's emotional, not just operational.** People don't avoid chasing because it's hard; they avoid it because it feels rude.
- Evidence: P5 "chasing clients feels rude so I put it off"; P2 owed $4k and "only noticed when rent was due"; P6 churned a client with aggressive reminders.
- Sources: 3/6 · **Strength: strong** (behavior + emotion + a real $ consequence)

**2. Freelancers build their own overdue-tracking workarounds.** The job "know who owes me and when to nudge" is unmet enough that people DIY it.
- Evidence: P4 built a weekend spreadsheet to flag overdue; P1 copies a Doc template each time.
- Sources: 2/6 · **Strength: moderate** (clear behavior, but only 2 sources)

**3. Reminder *tone* matters as much as reminders existing.** An existing tool (Wave) lost a user trust because reminders felt aggressive.
- Evidence: P6 "too aggressive, scared a client off."
- Sources: 1/6 · **Strength: weak** (single source — a hypothesis to test)

## Jobs & pains
- **Job:** "Get paid what I'm owed without damaging the client relationship."
- **Pains:** the awkwardness of chasing; losing track of who's overdue; one-off invoice creation friction.

## Surprises / disconfirming
- We expected invoice *creation* to be the pain; the stronger signal is *collection* and its emotional weight. P3's "AI that does my taxes" is off-topic scope-creep, not evidence for this problem.

## What we still don't know
- Whether the emotional-chasing pain generalizes beyond designers; n=6, convenience sample. Tone preference is a single data point. No data on willingness to pay.

## Suggested next steps
- Interview 8–10 more across freelance types, anchored on "the last time a client paid late," to test whether the *emotional* framing of chasing holds.
- Cheap test: prototype a "gentle nudge" reminder copy and test reactions — don't build the tracker yet.
