# Beachhead segment

The most common GTM mistake is going broad — "anyone could use this." A beachhead (Geoffrey Moore / Steve Blank) is the *one narrow segment you can dominate first*, because dominating a small pond gives you references, word-of-mouth, and focus that "a little bit of everyone" never does. This skill picks that segment against real criteria and argues why the broad/obvious option loses.

## The judgment this skill encodes

- **Narrow beats broad — aggressively.** Resist "everyone." A beachhead is a specific who (industry × size × role × situation), small enough to win and own. Winning 80% of a tiny market beats 1% of a huge one.
- **Score candidates on beachhead criteria:**
  - **Urgent, expensive pain** — they feel it now and will pay/switch.
  - **Reachable** — you can find and sell to them through a concrete channel.
  - **Referenceable / word-of-mouth** — they talk to each other; a win begets the next.
  - **Big enough** — supports a real business at the level you need.
  - **You can actually win it** — your product fits *their* needs best, vs. incumbents.
  - **Leads to adjacent markets** — a bridgehead you can expand from (the "bowling pin" next segments).
- **Pick one, and say no to the rest.** The output names the segment *and* explicitly de-prioritizes the tempting-but-worse ones (incl. the big obvious market), with reasons.
- **Beware the biggest-market trap.** The largest segment is usually the most competitive and least winnable for a newcomer — and "horizontal, for everyone" means resonating with no one.
- **Name the expansion path.** A good beachhead isn't a dead end; show the next 1–2 adjacent segments it unlocks.
- **Make it concrete enough to act on.** "SMBs" is not a beachhead; "dental practices with 1–3 dentists in the US who still use paper recall reminders" is.

## Process

1. **List candidate segments** (specific who × situation), including the broad/obvious one.
2. **Score each** on the criteria (urgent pain, reachable, referenceable, big-enough, winnable, leads-to-adjacent).
3. **Pick the beachhead** — the best-scoring narrow segment; make it concrete.
4. **Say no** — why the broad option and the runners-up are wrong *first*.
5. **Expansion path** — the adjacent segments this unlocks next.

## Output format

```
# <Product> — Beachhead Segment

## Candidate segments         (specific; incl. the broad/obvious option)
## Scoring                     | segment | urgent pain | reachable | referenceable | size | winnable | leads-to-adjacent |
## The beachhead               (the chosen narrow segment — concrete who × situation — and why)
## Why not the broad / runner-up   (explicit de-prioritization with reasons)
## Expansion path              (the next 1–2 adjacent segments it unlocks)
```

## Anti-patterns to refuse

- "Our market is everyone / it's horizontal" → Force one narrow, concrete segment.
- Picking the biggest market → Flag the competitiveness/winnability trap.
- A vague segment ("SMBs", "developers") → Make it concrete (who × size × situation).
- No expansion path → Show the adjacent segments it leads to.
- Listing segments without choosing → Pick one and say no to the rest.
