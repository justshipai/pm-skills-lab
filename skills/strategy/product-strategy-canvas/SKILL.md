# Product strategy canvas

A product strategy should fit on a page and answer: what future are we building, what do we believe that others don't, where will we focus, how will we win and keep winning, and how will we know? This skill assembles those into one coherent canvas. It draws on Roger Martin (strategy as choice) and Richard Rumelt (good strategy = diagnosis → guiding policy → coherent action), so the output is a real strategy, not a wish list with a Gantt chart attached.

For the deeper where-to-play/how-to-win choices, pair with `playing-to-win`; for the long-term picture, `product-vision`; for the metric, `north-star-metric`. This canvas ties them together.

## The judgment this skill encodes

- **Start with a diagnosis.** Rumelt's kernel: name the central challenge honestly before proposing anything. A strategy that skips the diagnosis is just ambition.
- **An insight, not a platitude.** State the non-obvious thing you believe about customers/market/tech that others don't — your reason to win. If everyone already believes it, it's not an edge.
- **Focus = saying no.** Name the few bets and, explicitly, what you're NOT doing. A strategy that funds everything funds nothing.
- **Defensibility, not just differentiation.** Say why your advantage persists (moat: network, switching cost, data, brand, integration). "We'll be better" decays.
- **Coherent actions.** The bets must follow from the diagnosis and insight — not a grab-bag. Each major initiative should trace to the guiding policy.
- **Measurable success.** How you'll know it's working (tie to a North Star / a few outcomes), with the assumptions that, if false, sink the strategy.

## Process

1. **Vision** (1 line — the future; or reference an existing one).
2. **Diagnosis** — the central challenge / current reality, honestly stated.
3. **Insight** — the non-obvious belief that gives you an edge.
4. **Where to focus** — the customer/segment/problem you're betting on; and what you're explicitly not doing.
5. **How to win & defend** — your advantage and why it lasts (the moat).
6. **The bets** — the few coherent initiatives that follow from the above.
7. **Success & assumptions** — the metric(s) that prove it's working and the what-must-be-true.

## Output format

```
# <Product> — Product Strategy Canvas

## Vision                  (one line)
## Diagnosis               (the central challenge, honestly)
## Insight                 (what we believe that others don't)
## Where to focus          (the bet — and explicit non-goals)
## How we win & defend     (advantage + why it persists / the moat)
## The bets                (the few coherent initiatives)
## Success & assumptions   (metric(s) + what must be true)
```

## Anti-patterns to refuse

- A roadmap or goals list labeled "strategy" → Insert diagnosis, insight, and focus.
- No diagnosis (jumps to solutions) → Name the central challenge first.
- An "insight" everyone already shares → Push for the non-obvious belief.
- Differentiation with no moat → Add why the advantage lasts.
- Bets that don't follow from the insight → Realign or cut them.
- Focus on everything → Force explicit non-goals.
