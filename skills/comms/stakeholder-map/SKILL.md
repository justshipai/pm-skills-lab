# Stakeholder map

Most cross-functional initiatives fail on alignment, not execution. This skill maps who can affect (or is affected by) your work onto a **Power × Interest grid**, then turns that map into an engagement plan: how much to involve each person, what they actually care about, and how/when to communicate. It also surfaces who's a champion and who's likely to block — the political reality a plain contact list ignores.

## The framework (Power × Interest grid)

- **High power, high interest → Manage closely.** Engage deeply, co-create, keep them bought in.
- **High power, low interest → Keep satisfied.** Don't over-communicate; give them what they need to stay supportive and not surprised.
- **Low power, high interest → Keep informed.** They care and can help (feedback, advocacy); update them regularly.
- **Low power, low interest → Monitor.** Light touch; watch for changes in their position.

## The judgment this skill encodes

- **Power and interest are different axes.** A senior exec may have high power but low interest; a frontline support lead may have low power but high interest and great insight. Place each deliberately.
- **Tailor the message to what they care about.** Engineering wants scope and feasibility; sales wants the customer story and dates; an exec wants the outcome and the risk. One update does not fit all.
- **Name champions and blockers explicitly.** Who will advocate, who's skeptical or threatened, and why. Plan to enlist champions and address blockers early — surprising a blocker in a review is how projects die.
- **Match cadence and channel to the quadrant.** Manage-closely → regular 1:1s/working sessions; keep-satisfied → concise periodic summaries; keep-informed → group updates; monitor → as-needed.
- **Stakeholders move.** A "keep satisfied" exec can become "manage closely" once the project hits their goals — revisit the map.
- **Don't confuse loudest with most important.** Map by actual power and stake, not by who emails the most.

## Process

1. **List stakeholders** (individuals/teams) who affect or are affected by the work.
2. **Rate each** on power and interest; place in a quadrant.
3. **For each:** what they care about, what they need from you, cadence, channel.
4. **Flag champions and blockers,** with the why and a plan for each.
5. **Summarize the engagement plan** and note who to revisit as the project evolves.

## Output format

```
# <Project> — Stakeholder Map

## Power × Interest grid
   - Manage closely:  <names>
   - Keep satisfied:  <names>
   - Keep informed:   <names>
   - Monitor:         <names>

## Engagement plan
   | Stakeholder | Quadrant | Cares about | Needs from us | Cadence | Channel |

## Champions & blockers     (who, why, plan)
## Revisit                   (whose position may shift)
```

## Anti-patterns to refuse

- A flat list of names with no power/interest placement → Put them on the grid.
- One communication plan for everyone → Tailor message, cadence, channel per quadrant.
- Ignoring blockers → Name them and plan to address them before the big review.
- Treating the loudest voice as the priority → Rank by real power and stake.
