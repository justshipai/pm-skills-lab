# Example: beachhead-segment

## Input

> We built an AI scheduling assistant. The founder says "everyone with a calendar
> is our market — it's horizontal." Where should we actually start?

## Output

# AI Scheduling Assistant — Beachhead Segment

## Candidate segments
- **"Everyone with a calendar"** (the founder's instinct).
- Sales teams booking demos.
- Recruiters scheduling interview loops.
- Consultants/coaches booking client sessions.
- Customer-success teams.

## Scoring
| Segment | Urgent pain | Reachable | Referenceable | Size | Winnable | Leads to adjacent |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Everyone w/ calendar | Low (diffuse) | No (no channel) | No | Huge | No (Calendly/Google own it) | — |
| Sales (demos) | High | Yes (sales communities, tools) | High (sales talks!) | Big | Med (crowded) | Yes |
| **Recruiters (interview loops)** | **High** (multi-person, reschedule hell) | **Yes** (ATS integrations, recruiter networks) | **High** (recruiters share tools) | Med-big | **Yes** (complex multi-party scheduling = our edge, underserved) | **Yes** (→ sales, CS) |
| Consultants | Med | Hard (fragmented) | Med | Med | Med | Limited |

## The beachhead
**In-house recruiters running multi-interviewer loops at mid-size companies.** Their pain is acute and specific — coordinating 3–5 interviewers + candidate across time zones, with constant reschedules — which is exactly where generic Calendly-style tools fall down and our multi-party logic wins. They're reachable (ATS integrations, recruiter communities) and highly referenceable (recruiters swap tool recommendations constantly).

## Why not the broad / runner-up
- **"Everyone with a calendar" loses:** no channel to reach them, no urgent shared pain, and Calendly/Google already own the simple 1:1 case. Horizontal = resonating with no one and competing with incumbents on their turf.
- **Sales (runner-up):** great pain and referenceability, but the most crowded scheduling niche (every sales tool bolts it on). Recruiting is similarly painful but *less contested* and plays to our multi-party strength — more winnable now. Revisit sales as expansion.

## Expansion path
Recruiters → **sales interview/demo scheduling** (adjacent multi-party need, warm from recruiter word-of-mouth) → **customer success** (recurring multi-party). The recruiting wedge proves the multi-party value, then we bowling-pin into the bigger, more contested segments from a position of strength.
