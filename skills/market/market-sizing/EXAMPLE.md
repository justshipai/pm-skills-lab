# Example: market-sizing

## Input

> Size the market for a SaaS tool that helps independent dental practices manage
> patient recalls and reminders, US only.

## Output

# Dental Recall SaaS (US) — Sizing

## Market definition
US **independent dental practices** (1-3 dentists) buying software to automate patient recall/reminder workflows. Excludes large DSO chains (different buyer) and non-US.

## Top-down
- ~200,000 dental practices in the US *(industry estimate)*.
- ~75% are independent/small (vs. DSO-owned) → ~150,000 *(assumption, sourced from dental-industry reports)*.
- ~60% would consider dedicated recall software vs. relying on their PMS → ~90,000 SAM practices.

## Bottom-up
- ~90,000 target practices × **$150/mo** plausible price (≈ $1,800/yr) = **~$162M/yr SAM**.
- Cross-check on willingness: recall automation recovers missed-appointment revenue (a no-show can cost $200+), so $150/mo is well within value — assumption looks sound.

## Reconciliation
Top-down practice count (90k) feeds the bottom-up directly, so they're consistent by construction here; the real uncertainty is **price** and **% who'd buy a dedicated tool vs. using their existing practice-management system**. Both flagged below.

## TAM / SAM / SOM
- **TAM:** all 150k independent practices × ~$1,800/yr ≈ **~$270M/yr**.
- **SAM:** ~90k reachable practices ≈ **~$160M/yr** (range **$120M–$200M** depending on price and adoption %).
- **SOM (3 yrs):** realistic to win low-single-digit % of SAM given incumbents and SMB GTM — ~**$5–12M ARR**, not "1% of TAM."

## Sensitivity
1. **Price** ($100 vs $200/mo) swings SAM ±35%.
2. **% who buy dedicated vs. rely on their PMS** — the biggest unknown; if PMS vendors bundle recall, SAM shrinks fast.
3. Independent-practice share (DSO consolidation is eroding it year over year).
