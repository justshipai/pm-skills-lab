# Example: porters-five-forces

## Input

> Run a Porter's Five Forces on the market for AI meeting-notes / transcription
> tools, where we're considering launching.

## Output

# AI Meeting-Notes Tools — Porter's Five Forces

## Market defined
SaaS tools that join/record meetings and produce transcripts + AI summaries/actions, sold to teams and individuals.

## Force-by-force
- **Competitive rivalry — pressure: HIGH.** Crowded with well-funded players plus features bolted into Zoom/Teams/Google. Low differentiation (everyone does "transcribe + summarize"), so it competes on price and distribution. Brutal.
- **Threat of new entrants — pressure: HIGH.** Low barriers: foundation models + speech APIs are commoditized, so a small team can ship a clone fast. Little structural moat unless you own distribution or proprietary data.
- **Supplier power — pressure: MEDIUM-HIGH.** You depend on a few model/speech API providers who set prices and could compete with you. Concentration of suppliers is a real margin and strategic risk.
- **Buyer power — pressure: HIGH.** Switching costs are low, free tiers everywhere, and the platform incumbents give a "good enough" version away bundled — so buyers are price-insensitive only until the bundled option appears, then they leave.
- **Threat of substitutes — pressure: HIGH.** The biggest threat isn't a rival app — it's **the feature baked into the meeting platform itself** (Zoom/Teams/Meet AI notes) and "just take notes yourself." Substitutes are strong and largely free.

## Overall read
**Structurally brutal.** Four of five forces are high, and the killer combo is *low entry barriers + powerful substitutes bundled by the platforms you run on top of*. Margins will be pressured hard; a standalone "transcribe + summarize" tool has weak structural defensibility.

## Implications for us
- **Don't enter on the commodity.** Competing head-on with "AI meeting notes" walks into all five forces at once.
- **Shift the forces or don't play:** the only defensible angles are (a) a vertical with proprietary workflow/data the platforms won't build (e.g. notes that flow into a specific industry's system of record), raising switching cost and reducing substitute threat; or (b) owning a distribution channel. Absent one of those, this is a market to avoid.
- **Supplier risk:** if we do enter, avoid single-provider lock-in and assume the model vendor may compete with us.
