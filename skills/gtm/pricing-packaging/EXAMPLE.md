# Example: pricing-packaging

## Input

> We built a tool that auto-generates social media posts for small businesses. It
> costs us about $2/customer/month to run. Someone said "let's charge $5/mo to get
> a healthy margin." How should we price and package it?

## Output

# Social Post Generator — Pricing & Packaging

## Value & the alternative
The customer's alternative is a freelancer/agency ($300–1,000/mo) or hours of their own time. Even saving a few hours a month is worth far more than $5. **Cost ($2) sets a floor, not the price** — pricing at $5 leaves almost all the value on the table.

## Value metric
Charge by a unit that scales with value: **posts (or connected social accounts/brands)**. A solo shop posting a little pays less; a busy multi-location business posting daily across channels pays more — the bill grows as they get more value. (Avoid a flat $5 that's identical for both.)

## Packaging (good-better-best)
- **Starter** — 1 brand, ~30 posts/mo, 2 channels. For solo businesses. (~$19/mo)
- **Pro** *(target)* — 3 brands, unlimited posts, all channels, scheduling, analytics. For active SMBs/freelancers. (~$49/mo)
- **Agency** — many brands, team seats, client management. (~$149/mo, or "contact us")

## Fences & expansion
Fences = number of brands, channels, scheduling/analytics, team seats. These map to intensity: as a business grows (more brands/channels/team), it naturally crosses a fence and upgrades — built-in expansion.

## Price points & anchoring
$19 / $49 / $149. The **Agency tier anchors up**, making Pro ($49) feel reasonable; Starter exists to capture price-sensitive solos without dragging the line down. Note: even Starter at $19 is ~10× cost — the margin worry is solved by value pricing, not by a $5 sticker.

## Willingness-to-pay check
Validate before locking: run a quick Van Westendorp / pricing survey with target SMBs, and benchmark against the freelancer alternative ($300+). Hypothesis: SMBs will pay $30–60/mo for the Pro outcome; confirm the Starter/Pro boundary doesn't cannibalize. Test the three tiers on the pricing page and watch tier mix.
