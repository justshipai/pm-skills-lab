# Product vision

A product vision describes the future you're trying to create for your customer — vivid enough to align a team for years, not a slogan and not a roadmap. Most attempts fail by being either empty ("be the best platform for X") or a disguised feature list. This skill writes a vision that passes the real test: someone reading it can tell what you believe, who it's for, and what the world looks like if you win.

## The judgment this skill encodes

- **Customer's world, not the company's.** Describe the change in the *customer's* life/work, not your market position or revenue. "Anyone can publish a book in a weekend," not "be the leading self-publishing platform."
- **Aspirational but believable.** Stretch far enough to inspire, anchored enough that the team believes it's reachable. Pure fantasy demotivates; timid visions don't move anyone.
- **Concrete over buzzwords.** Cut "seamless, innovative, world-class, AI-powered, next-generation." Paint a specific picture instead. If a competitor could paste their name into your vision, it's too generic.
- **Time horizon, loosely.** A vision is a multi-year destination (3–10 yrs), not this quarter. Make the timeframe felt, not dated.
- **Vision ≠ mission ≠ strategy.** Mission = why you exist; vision = the future you're building; strategy = how you'll get there. Keep the vision the *future state*, and don't let it collapse into a to-do list.
- **It should make trade-offs obvious.** A good vision helps a team say no — if an idea doesn't serve the vision, that should be visible.

## Process

1. **Who is it for** — the specific customer whose world changes.
2. **The change** — what's different/better in their world when you've succeeded (the heart of it).
3. **Why it matters** — the deeper human or business reason this future is worth building.
4. **Draft the vision statement** — a short, vivid paragraph (or a crisp one-liner + a paragraph that expands it).
5. **Scrub** — remove buzzwords and feature-talk; check the competitor-name test; confirm it's the customer's world, not yours.

## Output format

```
# <Product> — Product Vision

## Vision statement       (1–3 vivid sentences — the future, from the customer's world)
## The customer & the change   (who it's for; what's different when we win)
## Why it matters          (the deeper reason)
## What this rules in / out  (a few decisions the vision makes obvious)
```

## Anti-patterns to refuse

- "Be the #1 / leading / best-in-class platform for X" → Rewrite as the change in the customer's world.
- Buzzword stacks (seamless, innovative, AI-powered) → Replace with a concrete picture.
- A feature list or roadmap masquerading as vision → Lift it to the future state.
- A vision any competitor could also claim → Sharpen until it's unmistakably yours.
