# Example: competitive-battlecard

## Input

> Make a sales battlecard for our CRM (built for small service businesses) vs.
> Salesforce, which prospects keep bringing up.

## Output

# Battlecard: vs. Salesforce

## How we win (one line)
We're the CRM a small service business can set up this week and actually use — Salesforce is power they'll pay for, wait months to configure, and mostly never use.

## Their strengths → our reframe
| Acknowledge | Pivot |
|---|---|
| Endlessly customizable, huge ecosystem | "Powerful — if you have an admin and months to configure. Do you have someone to own that?" |
| The 'safe', known brand | "Great for a 500-person sales org. The question is whether it fits a 10-person service team without a Salesforce consultant." |

## Their weaknesses (exploitable)
- Time-to-value is long; SMBs routinely buy it and stall in implementation.
- Real cost = licenses + admin/consultant + add-ons — far above sticker.
- Overkill complexity → low adoption by non-salespeople (techs, owners).

## Our differentiators (that matter to this buyer)
- Live in a day, no consultant. Built around service workflows (jobs, scheduling, follow-ups), not enterprise sales pipelines. Flat, predictable price.

## Landmines — questions to plant
- "Ask them what it costs *fully set up for your workflow* — licenses plus implementation plus the admin to maintain it."
- "Ask how long until your team is actually using it day-to-day."
- "Ask who configures and maintains it after the sale."

## Objection handling
| They say… | We respond… |
|---|---|
| "But Salesforce can do anything." | "It can — that's the problem for a team your size. You'd pay for and maintain capability you won't use. We do the 20% you need, well, on day one." |
| "Salesforce is the safe choice." | "Safe for an enterprise. For a small team, the real risk is buying something you never fully adopt — that's the common Salesforce-for-SMB story." |

## When we win / When to walk away
- **Win:** small/mid service businesses, no dedicated ops/admin, want fast time-to-value and service-shaped workflows.
- **Walk away:** prospects with complex multi-team sales processes, an existing Salesforce admin, or deep ecosystem dependencies — that's their deal; don't burn the cycle.
