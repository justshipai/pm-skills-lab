# Example: messaging-house

## Input

> Build the messaging for our product. It's an AI-powered customer-support tool.
> Marketing keeps writing feature lists ("AI replies, macros, analytics,
> integrations") and a tagline "Support, supercharged." Give us real messaging.

## Output

# AI Support Tool — Messaging House

## Core message (roof)
**Resolve more support tickets in less time — without your team drowning or your customers feeling like they're talking to a bot.** (Benefit + the dual fear it removes; not "Support, supercharged," which says nothing.)

## Pillars (the walls)
**Pillar 1 — Your team handles more, with less effort.**
→ *Proof:* AI drafts replies agents edit-and-send (cuts handle time); macros and triage automate the routine. Data: pilot teams resolved X% more tickets/agent.

**Pillar 2 — Customers get faster, accurate answers (that still feel human).**
→ *Proof:* answers grounded in your help docs with citations; agent review keeps quality; CSAT held/up in pilots.

**Pillar 3 — You can see and improve what's happening.**
→ *Proof:* analytics on volume, resolution time, deflection; spot trends and gaps. (Analytics is proof here, not a pillar by itself.)

*(Integrations aren't a pillar — they're proof under "less effort"/"fits your stack.")*

## Per-audience translation
- **Support leader (buyer):** "Cut handle time and grow capacity without hiring" — emphasize Pillars 1 & 3 (efficiency + visibility), ROI.
- **Agent (user):** "Spend less time typing the same answers, more on hard cases" — emphasize Pillar 1, and that they stay in control (edit before send).
- **Exec:** "Scale support cost-efficiently while protecting CSAT" — roof + Pillar 2's quality guardrail.

## Gaps
- Pillar 2's "feels human / quality" needs stronger proof — get a CSAT-vs-control number before leaning on it.
- Confirm the "X% more tickets" stat is real and citable; if not, soften to a directional claim.
- Everything rolls up to the roof (more resolved, less drowning, still human) — no orphan messages. The old feature list and tagline are retired.
