# Example: playing-to-win

## Input

> Help me set strategy for our scheduling app. We're a small player against
> Calendly and others. We want to grow but feel stuck competing on features.

## Output

# Scheduling App — Strategy Cascade (Playing to Win)

## 1. Winning aspiration
Become the default scheduling layer for **regulated service businesses** (clinics, law firms, financial advisors) — winning means being the tool they trust with compliance-sensitive bookings, not the broadest calendar tool.

## 2. Where to play
- **Play:** small/mid regulated service providers (healthcare, legal, financial) in markets with strict privacy/record rules; sold through industry-specific channels and integrations (EHRs, practice-management tools).
- **Won't play:** the horizontal "anyone who books meetings" market Calendly owns; consumer/individual free users; enterprise sales-team scheduling. We deliberately give up volume to own a defensible niche.

## 3. How to win
Win on **compliance and trust, deeply built in** — HIPAA/records-grade data handling, consent capture, audit trails, and integrations with the systems these businesses already must use. This is defensible because (a) horizontal players won't compromise their simple UX to add heavy compliance, and (b) the niche integrations + certifications are slow and unglamorous to build — exactly the moat generalists avoid.

## 4. Capabilities
- Deep compliance/security engineering and certifications (HIPAA, SOC 2, regional rules).
- Integrations with vertical systems (EHR/practice management).
- Domain-credible go-to-market (people who speak "clinic ops," not "growth hacks").

## 5. Management systems
- A compliance roadmap treated as a product surface, not a checkbox.
- Metrics centered on regulated-vertical activation & retention (not raw signups).
- A partnerships function for vertical integrations; security reviews built into shipping.

## Coherence & what must be true
The choices reinforce: the niche (where) needs compliance (how), which needs the security capabilities and vertical GTM, which the systems sustain. **What must be true:** (1) regulated providers feel real pain with horizontal tools' compliance gaps and will switch for it; (2) the compliance moat is genuinely hard for Calendly to replicate without hurting their core simplicity; (3) the niche is big enough to grow into. The first is the riskiest — validate it with regulated-provider interviews before over-investing.
