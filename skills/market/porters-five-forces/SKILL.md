# Porter's Five Forces

Porter's Five Forces explains *why* an industry is profitable or brutal — the structural forces, not just who the competitors are. The classic misuse is to treat it as "list the rivals." This skill works all five forces, rates each (high/medium/low pressure) with evidence, and — crucially — turns the analysis into strategic implications: where the profit pressure comes from and what we should do about it.

## The five forces

1. **Competitive rivalry** — intensity among existing players (number, growth, differentiation, exit barriers).
2. **Threat of new entrants** — how easily newcomers can enter (barriers: capital, network effects, regulation, brand, switching costs).
3. **Supplier power** — leverage of your inputs/suppliers (concentration, switching cost, criticality).
4. **Buyer power** — leverage of your customers (concentration, price sensitivity, switching cost, information).
5. **Threat of substitutes** — alternatives that solve the same need a different way (incl. "do it manually / not at all").

## The judgment this skill encodes

- **All five, not just rivalry.** The interesting threats usually hide in substitutes, buyer power, or entrants — not the obvious rival list.
- **Rate each force and say why.** High / medium / low *pressure*, with the structural reason. An unrated force is an unfinished thought.
- **Substitutes ≠ competitors.** A substitute solves the job differently (spreadsheets vs. your SaaS, "hire an agency" vs. your tool). These are easy to miss and often the real threat.
- **It's about the industry's profitability, then yours.** First read the structure; then where *you* sit in it and how you can shift a force in your favor (raise entry barriers, reduce buyer power via lock-in, etc.).
- **End with the "so what."** Which forces most threaten margins, and the strategic responses. Analysis with no implication is a textbook exercise.
- **Evidence over assertion.** Tie each rating to observable structure, and flag what's inferred.

## Process

1. **Define the industry/market** being analyzed (precisely — sizing-style).
2. **Work each of the five forces** — the structural drivers, a high/med/low rating, and why.
3. **Overall read** — is this a structurally attractive market or a hard one? Where's the profit pressure?
4. **Implications for us** — which forces to worry about, and how to shift them in our favor.

## Output format

```
# <Market> — Porter's Five Forces

## Market defined
## Force-by-force
   - **Competitive rivalry** — pressure: H/M/L — drivers & why
   - **Threat of new entrants** — pressure: H/M/L — barriers & why
   - **Supplier power** — pressure: H/M/L — why
   - **Buyer power** — pressure: H/M/L — why
   - **Threat of substitutes** — pressure: H/M/L — what they are & why
## Overall read              (attractive or brutal? where's the margin pressure?)
## Implications for us        (which forces to act on; how to shift them)
```

## Anti-patterns to refuse

- Listing competitors instead of analyzing forces → Work all five structural forces.
- Skipping substitutes or buyer power → Force every one.
- Ratings with no reasoning → Give the structural driver behind each H/M/L.
- No "so what" → End with which forces threaten margins and the strategic response.
