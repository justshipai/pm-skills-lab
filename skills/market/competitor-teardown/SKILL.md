# Competitor teardown

A competitor analysis is useless if it stops at "here's what they have." The point is to understand their strategy well enough to decide what *we* should do. This skill produces a teardown that reads their positioning and business model, separates real strengths from noise, finds where they're genuinely vulnerable, and ends with the "so what for us" — specific, defensible implications, not a feature checklist.

## The judgment this skill encodes

- **Strategy over feature inventory.** Don't list everything they ship — explain *who they're for, how they win, and how they make money.* Features matter only as evidence of strategy.
- **Honest strengths, real weaknesses.** Acknowledge what they're genuinely good at (wishful thinking loses deals), and find structural weaknesses — not cosmetic gripes but things hard for them to fix (their business model, their core customer, their tech debt, their incentives).
- **Where they win and lose, and why.** In which segments/use cases does this competitor beat us, and where do they fall down? Be specific about the conditions.
- **Always end with "so what for us."** Every teardown closes with implications and concrete moves: where to avoid head-on, where to attack, what to copy, what to ignore. A teardown with no recommendation is trivia.
- **Evidence, not assertion.** Base claims on observable signals (pricing pages, positioning, reviews, hiring, launches), and flag where you're inferring.
- **Don't mistake big for invincible (or small for harmless).** Size cuts both ways — incumbents have inertia and a core to protect; challengers have focus. Read the structural position, not the logo.

## Process

1. **Snapshot** — who they are, target customer, positioning in one tight paragraph.
2. **Business model** — how they make money, pricing/packaging, motion (PLG/sales).
3. **Strengths** — what they're genuinely good at (be honest).
4. **Weaknesses** — structural vulnerabilities, not cosmetic.
5. **Where they win / where they lose** — segments & use cases, with conditions.
6. **Implications for us** — concrete moves: avoid / attack / copy / ignore, and the bet it points to.

## Output format

```
# <Competitor> — Teardown

## Snapshot                  (who, who-for, positioning)
## Business model            (monetization, pricing, motion)
## Strengths (honest)
## Weaknesses (structural)
## Where they win / where they lose   (segments & use cases + conditions)
## Implications for us        (avoid / attack / copy / ignore — concrete moves)
## Confidence & gaps          (what's evidenced vs. inferred; what to dig into)
```

## Anti-patterns to refuse

- A neutral feature inventory → Reframe around strategy and end with implications.
- Only listing weaknesses (wishful) or only strengths (defeatist) → Give an honest both.
- Cosmetic weaknesses ("their UI is dated") as the headline → Find structural ones.
- No "so what for us" → Add concrete moves; that's the deliverable.
- Asserting without evidence → Mark inferences and name what to verify.
