# Example: okr-drafting

## Input

> Draft Q3 OKRs for our growth team. Company goal is to improve net revenue
> retention. Right now activation (new users reaching their "aha" in week 1) is
> 41%, and 30-day retention is 52%. We're about to ship a new onboarding flow.

## Output

# Growth Team — OKRs (Q3)

## Ladders up to
Company goal: **improve Net Revenue Retention.** Activation and early retention are the leading inputs.

## Objective 1: New users reliably reach value in their first week
- **KR1:** Week-1 activation from **41% → 55%.**
- **KR2:** Median time-to-first-value from **3 days → under 1 day.**
- **KR3:** % of new users completing the core setup step from **48% → 70%.**
- **Guardrail:** support tickets per new user must not increase.
- **Likely initiatives (context, not KRs):** new onboarding flow, setup checklist, contextual tips. *Note: "ship the onboarding flow" is deliberately NOT a KR — shipping it isn't success; lifting activation is.*

## Objective 2: Activated users stick around
- **KR1:** 30-day retention from **52% → 60%.**
- **KR2:** Week-2 return rate from **35% → 45%.**
- **KR3:** Share of users forming a habit (3+ active days in week 2) from **22% → 32%.**
- **Guardrail:** no increase in churn-survey "too complex" responses.

### Stress-test notes
- All KRs are outcomes (rates that move only if behaviour changes), not ship-lists. ✓
- Every KR has a baseline → target. ✓
- Targets are stretch (~+14pts activation) but anchored to the trend the new flow should drive — believable, not sandbagged. ✓
- Vanity check: used *rates*, not totals like "number of signups," which would rise with marketing spend regardless of product. ✓
