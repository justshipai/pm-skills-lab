# Competitive battlecard

A battlecard is a weapon for a rep mid-deal, not an analyst document. It has to be fast, honest, and usable under pressure: what to say when the buyer is also evaluating this competitor. This skill produces a card a salesperson can actually use — leading with how to win, arming them with traps to set and objections to handle, and (crucially) telling them when to walk away rather than waste the cycle.

## The judgment this skill encodes

- **Lead with the one-liner.** "How we beat them in a sentence" up top — a rep scanning before a call needs the headline, then the supporting detail.
- **Acknowledge their strengths honestly.** Reps lose trust (with buyers and themselves) if the card pretends the competitor is bad. Name what's genuinely good, then pivot to what it costs the buyer.
- **Weaknesses must be exploitable in a deal.** Not "their roadmap is unclear" — concrete gaps the buyer will feel, that the rep can surface.
- **Set landmines (discovery questions).** The strongest move is questions the rep asks that lead the buyer to discover the competitor's weakness themselves — "ask them how X works when Y." More persuasive than claims.
- **Objection handling, buyer's words.** For each likely "but Competitor does Z," a crisp, non-defensive response that reframes.
- **Say when to walk away.** Be honest about the deals this competitor should win — chasing unwinnable deals burns the team. A battlecard that claims you win everything is useless.
- **No trash-talk.** Confident and factual beats smug; disparaging competitors backfires with buyers.

## Process

1. **One-line how-to-win** against this competitor.
2. **Their strengths** (honest) and how to neutralize/reframe each.
3. **Their weaknesses** the buyer will feel — exploitable in-deal.
4. **Our differentiators** that matter to this buyer.
5. **Landmines** — discovery questions that surface their gaps.
6. **Objection handling** — top "but they do X" + the response.
7. **When we win / when to walk** — the deal shapes for each.

## Output format

```
# Battlecard: vs. <Competitor>

## How we win (one line)
## Their strengths → our reframe   (acknowledge | pivot)
## Their weaknesses (exploitable)
## Our differentiators (that matter to this buyer)
## Landmines — questions to plant   ("Ask them how…")
## Objection handling              | "They say…" | "We respond…" |
## When we win  /  When to walk away
```

## Anti-patterns to refuse

- A neutral feature-comparison table → Reframe as a how-to-win sales tool.
- Pretending the competitor has no strengths → Acknowledge, then reframe.
- Vague weaknesses a rep can't use → Make them buyer-felt and surfaceable.
- Claims with no landmine questions → Add discovery questions that let the buyer find the gap.
- "We win every deal" → Include honest when-to-walk-away.
- Trash-talk → Keep it confident and factual.
