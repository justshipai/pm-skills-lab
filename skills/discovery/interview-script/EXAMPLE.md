# Example: interview-script

## Input

> We're thinking about building a budgeting feature in our banking app. Write
> questions to interview users about budgeting.

## Output

# Personal Budgeting — Discovery Interview Script

## Learning goal
Decide whether budgeting is a problem worth solving in-app, and *how* people actually try to manage their money today. We need to learn their real behaviour and workarounds — not whether they'd like a budgeting feature (everyone says yes).

## Warm-up
- "Walk me through how you checked your money this week — what did you open, and when?"
- "Roughly how do you think about your spending month to month?"

## The story (anchored to a recent instance)
- "Tell me about the **last time** you worried you might overspend. What was going on?"
- "What did you actually do about it — step by step?"
- "Were you using anything to keep track — an app, a spreadsheet, your head, a partner? Show me if you can."
- "Tell me about the last time that approach let you down. What happened?"
- "When was the last time you changed your spending because of something you saw? What did you see, and what did you do?"

## Probes (go deeper, don't lead)
- "What did you do next?" · "Why did that matter to you?" · "How did you feel at that point?" · "What would have happened if you'd done nothing?"

## Solution reactions (only if time, clearly fenced)
- "I'm going to describe a rough idea — react honestly, including everything you'd hate about it." *(Keep last; do not let it colour the earlier story.)*

## Wrap-up
- "If we talked again in a month, what would you hope had changed about managing your money?"
- "Who else should I talk to about this?"

## Interviewer notes
- **Listen for:** the workaround they already built (spreadsheet, mental rules, separate account), the trigger moment, and the emotional language ("stressed," "guilty").
- **Avoid saying:** "Would you use…", "Do you think a budgeting feature would help…", "How often do you wish…". These invite agreement, not truth.
