# Weekly update

A status update exists to keep busy stakeholders aligned and to get the PM what they need (decisions, unblocks) — not to prove the team was busy. The default failure is a task log: "did X, worked on Y, started Z." This skill writes an update that leads with **outcomes and what changed**, surfaces the **blockers that need someone**, and respects the reader's 30 seconds.

## The judgment this skill encodes

- **Lead with outcomes, not activity.** "Activation is up 4pts after the new onboarding shipped" beats "worked on onboarding." If a line doesn't tell the reader what *changed* or what's *at stake*, cut or rewrite it.
- **Make blockers and asks impossible to miss.** The most valuable part of an update is "here's what I need a decision on." Put it where a skimming exec will see it.
- **Scannable structure.** Short sections, bold the headline of each, lead each bullet with the point. Assume it's read on a phone in 30 seconds.
- **Status with honesty.** On-track / at-risk / off-track, stated plainly. Hiding a slip until it's a crisis destroys trust; flag risk early with the plan.
- **Right altitude for the audience.** Execs want outcomes, risks, and decisions; the team wants detail. Don't bury an exec in ticket-level detail — match the altitude, or note who each section is for.
- **Forward-looking.** What's next and by when, so the reader knows what to expect — not just a rear-view mirror.

## Process

1. **Pull the signal from the activity** — what actually moved, shipped, or changed; drop pure busywork.
2. **State overall status** (on-track / at-risk / off-track) in one line.
3. **Write the sections:** Shipped & impact → In progress (with timeline) → Blockers / decisions needed → Next.
4. **Surface asks** — make the decisions/unblocks needed explicit and assigned.
5. **Tighten for scannability** — lead-with-the-point bullets, bolded headlines, cut filler.

## Output format

```
# <Team/Project> — Weekly Update (<date>)

**Status:** 🟢 on-track / 🟡 at-risk / 🔴 off-track — <one-line why>

## Shipped & impact          (what changed for users/business, with numbers if any)
## In progress                (what's moving + expected timing)
## ⚠ Blockers / decisions needed   (the ask, who from, by when)
## Next                        (what to expect next period)
```

## Anti-patterns to refuse

- A task log ("worked on X, did Y") → Rewrite each line as the outcome or what's at stake.
- Blockers buried at the bottom or omitted → Pull them up and make the ask explicit.
- Wall of ticket-level detail for an exec audience → Raise the altitude.
- "On track" when it isn't → State at-risk/off-track honestly with the plan.
- No "what's next" → Add the forward look.
