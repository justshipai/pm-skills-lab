# Interview script

Most interview questions are quietly useless: they're leading ("Would you use a feature that…?"), hypothetical ("Would you pay for…?"), or solution-first — and they produce confirmation, not insight. This skill writes a discovery script that follows the one rule that makes interviews worth running: **ask about specific past behavior, not opinions or futures.** Grounded in Jobs-to-be-Done and Teresa Torres' continuous-discovery interviewing.

## The judgment this skill encodes

- **Past behavior over speculation.** "Tell me about the last time you…" beats "Would you…?" People are terrible at predicting their own behavior and great at recounting it. Anchor questions to a specific recent instance.
- **Non-leading, open questions.** No question that hints at the answer or smuggles in your solution. Strip "would you like," "do you think it'd be useful," "how often do you wish."
- **Dig for the story.** Get the who/when/what-triggered-it/what-they-did-next, including the workarounds and the emotional moments. The job lives in the details.
- **Separate the goal from the questions.** State what you're trying to learn; every question should ladder to it. Cut questions that don't.
- **Don't pitch.** A discovery interview is not a demo. Save reactions to solutions for the end, clearly fenced off, if at all.
- **Plan the open and the probes.** A warm-up that gets them telling stories, and a set of follow-up probes ("what did you do then?", "why did that matter?") to go deeper without leading.

## Process

1. **Name the learning goal** — the decision this research informs and what you need to learn.
2. **Pick the anchor moment** — the specific recent experience you'll have them recount.
3. **Write the script:** warm-up → the story (past-behavior questions) → probes → (optional, fenced) solution reactions → wrap-up.
4. **Scrub for leading/hypothetical questions** and rewrite them as past-behavior.
5. **Add interviewer notes:** what to listen for, what to avoid saying.

## Output format

```
# <Topic> — Discovery Interview Script

## Learning goal            (the decision this informs; what we need to learn)
## Warm-up                   (1–2 easy, rapport-building questions)
## The story                 (past-behavior questions anchored to a recent instance)
## Probes                    (follow-ups to go deeper: "what did you do next?", "why?")
## Solution reactions        (optional, clearly fenced — only at the end)
## Wrap-up
## Interviewer notes         (what to listen for; phrases to avoid)
```

## Anti-patterns to refuse

- "Would you use / would you pay / do you think X is useful?" → Rewrite as "tell me about the last time you…".
- Questions that describe your solution before asking → Remove or fence to the end.
- A flat question list with no anchor moment → Anchor to a specific recent experience.
- Yes/no questions → Make them open and story-eliciting.
