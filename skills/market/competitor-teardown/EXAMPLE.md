# Example: competitor-teardown

## Input

> Tear down our main competitor. We make an email marketing tool for small
> e-commerce shops; the competitor is the big all-in-one platform most of them
> "graduate" into.

## Output

# [Big All-in-One Platform] — Teardown

## Snapshot
An all-in-one marketing + e-commerce platform targeting growing SMB merchants who want "everything in one place." Positions on breadth and being the system you scale with. Our shared customer is the small shop deciding between a focused tool (us) and the suite (them).

## Business model
Tiered SaaS that scales with contacts/features; lands cheap, expands as merchants grow and get locked into the suite. Sales-assisted at higher tiers, self-serve at entry. Revenue grows with customer size → strong incentive to push merchants up-tier.

## Strengths (honest)
- Breadth: email + automation + store + CRM in one — genuinely convenient for merchants who don't want to integrate tools.
- Switching cost: once a merchant's store + data + automations live there, leaving is painful. This is their real moat.
- Brand/distribution: most of our customers have heard of them.

## Weaknesses (structural)
- Breadth → shallowness: each module is "fine," none is best-in-class. Email power-users hit ceilings.
- Pricing balloons as contacts grow — a recurring, structural pain point (their model *requires* it).
- They can't get too focused on small shops' email needs without undercutting their up-tier expansion motion — an incentive they can't easily escape.

## Where they win / where they lose
- **Win:** merchants who value one-vendor simplicity over depth; those already on their store product.
- **Lose:** email-sophisticated shops frustrated by shallow automation and ballooning cost; merchants who already have a store they like and just want great email.

## Implications for us
- **Avoid:** competing on breadth — we'll lose. Don't build a store/CRM to match.
- **Attack:** their two structural sore spots — *email depth* and *price-at-scale*. Lead with "best-in-class email without the all-in-one tax."
- **Copy:** their frictionless entry onboarding.
- **Ignore:** their CRM module — not our game.
- **Bet it points to:** win the email-serious small shop who resents paying suite prices for mediocre email; make migration *out* of their suite (for email) painless.

## Confidence & gaps
Pricing/positioning claims are from public pages and reviews (solid). "Automation is shallow" is inferred from reviews — verify with a hands-on trial and a few win/loss interviews.
