# Example: product-strategy-canvas

## Input

> Put our product strategy on one page. We're a mid-size HR software company; our
> core hiring product is growing slowly and a few AI-native startups are nipping
> at us.

## Output

# HR Suite — Product Strategy Canvas

## Vision
Hiring teams make better decisions faster, with the busywork gone — so they spend their time on people, not process.

## Diagnosis
Our hiring product grew on being the *system of record* (workflows, compliance, integrations). But buyers increasingly judge us on *day-to-day usefulness*, where AI-native startups feel magical. Our growth is slowing because our moat (data + integrations) is invisible to the user evaluating us on a demo, while our daily UX feels dated. The central challenge: **we own the data and the workflow, but not the daily delight — and we're being out-demoed.**

## Insight
The AI-native challengers can build a slick assistant, but they **don't have the customer's historical hiring data or the system-of-record integrations** — and that data is what makes AI assistance actually useful (and trustworthy) rather than generic. We believe the durable AI advantage in HR comes from *proprietary, in-context data*, not from the model. Most competitors are betting the opposite.

## Where to focus
Bet on **AI assistance built on our system-of-record data** for mid-market recruiting teams. **Not doing:** chasing standalone-assistant feature parity with no data moat; expanding into adjacent HR modules (payroll, L&D) this year; SMB self-serve.

## How we win & defend
Win by making our AI uniquely useful *because* it's grounded in the customer's own hiring history and connected systems — surfacing candidates, drafting role-specific outreach, flagging bias using their data. **Defensible** via switching cost (the data lives with us) and integration depth a startup can't replicate quickly. The moat is the data + integrations, finally made visible through AI.

## The bets
1. An AI hiring assistant grounded in the customer's own data (not a bolt-on chatbot).
2. Modernize the daily recruiter UX so the demo no longer loses to challengers.
3. Deepen the few integrations that feed the data advantage.
*(Each follows from the insight — turning invisible data/integration moat into visible daily value.)*

## Success & assumptions
- **Success:** weekly active recruiters using AI-assisted actions (North Star), win-rate vs. AI-native challengers in competitive deals, mid-market retention.
- **What must be true:** (1) our data genuinely makes AI assistance better enough that users feel it; (2) we can modernize UX fast enough to stop the demo bleed; (3) buyers will value data-grounded trust over standalone flash. (1) and (2) are the riskiest — validate (1) with a grounded-assistant prototype before committing the roadmap.
